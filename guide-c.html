Why the same medication has so many different prices

Compounded weight-loss medication is prepared by licensed compounding pharmacies rather than sold as a branded product. What you pay is set by the provider you go through, not by a single list price, which is why two programs can quote very different numbers for a similar treatment.

The spread comes down to a few things: how much the provider marks up the pharmacy cost, whether clinician review and shipping are bundled in, and whether the price is tied to your dose. None of those are visible from an advert, which is why it pays to look at the monthly figure and what sits behind it.

What the monthly price usually includes

Most online programs quote one monthly figure covering the medication, a licensed clinician's review of your health questionnaire, and delivery to your door. Some add consultation fees, shipping or dose changes on top. It is worth reading which model you are looking at.

Does the price change as the dose goes up?

On many programs it does. Dosing for these medications usually steps up over the first months, and some providers charge more at each step. Others hold the same monthly price at every dose. This is one of the largest differences between programs over a year.

Is there a joining fee or a minimum term?

Some programs ask you to commit to several months up front. Others bill month to month with no minimum. Neither is automatically better, but a low first-month price attached to a long commitment can cost more overall than a flat monthly rate.

What about insurance?

Compounded medication is generally not covered by insurance, which is part of why programs quote a direct monthly price. It also means the figure you are quoted is usually the figure you pay.

How getting started works

The process is the same across most providers. You complete a health questionnaire online, covering your history, current medications and what you have already tried. A licensed clinician reviews it and decides whether a prescription is appropriate. If it is, the medication is dispensed by a pharmacy and delivered to you.

There is usually no appointment to book and no waiting room. The review is typically done within a day.

Who these programs are for

Many programs consider adults with a BMI of around 27 and above, and the questionnaire asks about conditions and medications that may rule treatment out. The clinician decides. Plenty of people assume they would not qualify and never check.

Common questions

Is it the same as the branded medication?

Compounded preparations are made by licensed compounding pharmacies and are a different product from branded medications. A clinician can explain what that means for your situation.

What if I am not approved?

Programs differ, but most do not charge for medication that is never dispensed. Check the provider's policy before you pay.

How quickly does it arrive?

Once a prescription is approved, most programs ship within a few days.

Can I stop?

That depends on the program. Some bill month to month, others ask for a minimum term. It is worth checking before you start.
